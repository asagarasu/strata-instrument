# Haiku cluster manifest: Bashō's 古池や and 閑さや — sources and translations

Compiled 2026-07-05. Raw gathering, locating, and cross-checking only —
no passage selection performed. Two target poems throughout:

- **P1** 古池や蛙飛びこむ水の音 (furuike ya / kawazu tobikomu / mizu no
  oto — the "old pond" hokku, 1686)
- **P2** 閑さや岩にしみ入る蝉の声 (shizukasa ya / iwa ni shimiiru / semi
  no koe — the "stillness/cicada" hokku, composed 1689, published in
  『おくのほそ道』 1702)

All positive claims below were established by reading/searching the
actual fetched texts, not by trusting secondary "known translations"
lists; where a claim rests on a secondary source only, that is said
explicitly.

## Textual variants found (checked, not assumed)

**P1 (old pond):** (a) The 1903 edition fetched prints 蛙**とびこむ**
(kana); the canonical modern form (from 『春の日』, 1686) is 蛙飛びこむ —
orthographic only. (b) Genuine draft/variant history per ja.wikipedia
(consulted 2026-07-05): initial draft 「古池や蛙**飛ンだる**水の音」 in
『庵桜』 (early 1686, Danrin-style); disciple 其角 proposed 山吹や for
the opening five, rejected by Bashō, but a transmitted
「**山吹や**蛙飛び込む水の音」 text survives in a 1700 collection. First
publication 『蛙合』 (3rd month 1686), then 『春の日』 (8th month 1686).

**P2 (stillness/cicada):** (a) The fetched Oku no Hosomichi edition
prints main text 岩に**しみ込**せみの聲 with apparatus note
「一本**しみ入る**トアリ」 — i.e. the manuscript tradition genuinely
splits, and this base text's main reading is NOT the today-famous
しみ入る form. Two further apparatus entries in the same passage
(にして/しノ字ナシ; 勝閑/淸簡). (b) The 1903 芭蕉俳句全集 page fetched
for P1 *also* contains P2 (夏/蟬 section, headnote 立石亭) reading
**しみ入る** — so the corpus's two Japanese witnesses split exactly
along the variant line. (c) Composition history per ja.wikipedia:
曾良's notebook records the on-site draft 「山寺や石にしみつく蝉の声」;
an intermediate 「さびしさや岩にしみ込む蝉の声」 is also reported;
final form 閑さや….

## Located only (in copyright — for later 2-14 line human excerpting)

### R. H. Blyth (d. 1964)

- **P1, old pond.** Blyth's standard rendering ("The old pond; / A
  frog jumps in — / The sound of the water.") is quoted without
  citation at https://www.bopsecrets.org/gateway/passages/basho-frog.htm .
  Confirmed carrier volumes/locations:
  - *A History of Haiku*, Vol. 2 (Tokyo: Hokuseido, 1964), pp. 46-76 —
    the chapter translating Shiki's 1898 essay "Shiki on Furu-ike ya"
    (citation verified from Geoffrey Wilkinson, "Bashō's frog, the
    great survivor," *Frogpond* 41:2 (2018), fn. 3, which cites the
    1969 printing). Archive.org lending copy:
    https://archive.org/details/historyofhaikuvo0002rhbl
  - *Zen in English Literature and Oriental Classics* (Tokyo:
    Hokuseido, 1942) devotes several pages of explication to this
    verse (per https://www.firstofthemonth.org/on-the-road-with-r-h-blyth/ ).
  - The 4-volume *Haiku* (Hokuseido, 1949-52): the frog is a spring
    kigo, so Vol. 2 *Spring* (1950) is the expected location, **but I
    could not pin the exact volume/page** — Archive.org's full-text
    search endpoint for the lending copies returned "item not
    available," and no reliable secondary page citation was found.
    Lending copies for human follow-up:
    https://archive.org/details/haiku0002rhbl (1950),
    https://archive.org/details/haiku0002blyt (1981 reprint).
- **P2, cicada.** Reported location: *Haiku*, Vol. 3: *Summer-Autumn*
  (Hokuseido, 1952), **pp. 816-818**, rendering reported as "The
  silence; / The voice of the cicadas / Penetrate[s] the rocks."
  CAVEAT: this page citation and wording come from a secondary web
  source surfaced in search results and were NOT verified against the
  volume itself. Lending copy: https://archive.org/details/haiku0003rhbl ;
  Vol. 1 (1960 printing): https://archive.org/details/haikuinfourvolum0001rhbl .
  The Haiku Foundation Digital Library has catalog records for Vols. 2
  and 3 (https://thehaikufoundation.org/omeka/items/show/221 , /222)
  but their PDF files are withdrawn ("PDF NOT AVAILABLE" — confirmed:
  the file URL returns an HTML error page, consistent with the
  in-copyright status).

## Gaps (honest NOT-FOUND record)

1. **No public-domain English translation of P2 (閑さや) found.** Both
   pre-1929 candidates were fetched and full-text searched: Aston 1899
   and Chamberlain 1902 each translate a *different* Bashō cicada poem
   (やがて死ぬけしきは見えず蝉の声 — Aston p. 296 area / Chamberlain
   epigram 80, p. ~330) but **not** 閑さや. Do not mistake those for
   P2. A PD English P2 would need another pre-1929 source not in this
   cluster.
2. **周作人 rendering of P2: NOT FOUND.** His 1923 「日本的小诗」 (fetched)
   contains P1 but no P2; targeted Chinese-web searches for a Zhou
   version of 閑さや found none — translation roundups of P2 (e.g.
   zhihu p/142460256) list 林林 and others but not Zhou. Marked NOT
   FOUND rather than substituting another translator.
3. **Zhou wording uncertainty for P1:** the fetched full text reads
   「古池呀——青蛙跳入水里的声音。」; some secondary lists quote
   「古池——青蛙跳进水里的声音」 (跳进, no 呀). Unresolved without a
   scanned printed 《艺术与生活》; recorded in the file header.
4. **Zhou has an earlier haiku essay,** 《日本之俳句》 (1916, per 刘晓颖,
   信阳师院学报 paper on Bashō translation history) — possibly an even
   earlier carrier of a P1 rendering. Full text not located; lead only,
   not fetched.
5. **Blyth precision gaps:** exact page for P1 in the 4-volume *Haiku*
   not pinned; the Vol. 3 pp. 816-818 citation for P2 is
   secondary-source-only (see above). *A History of Haiku* Vol. 2
   pp. 46-76 for P1 is the one Blyth pointer verified from a citable
   scholarly footnote.
6. **diancang.xyz DOM scramble** (see zh_target header): paragraph
   sequence in the Zhou file is not reading order. Content complete,
   order not trustworthy; the P1 passage itself is a self-contained
   paragraph and unaffected.
