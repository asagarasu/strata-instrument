NEGLECTED

Green grows the grass upon the bank,
The willow-shoots are long and lank;
A lady in a glistening gown
Opens the casement and looks down.
The roses on her cheek blush bright,
Her rounded arm is dazzling white;
A singing-girl in early life,
And now a careless roué's wife....
Ah, if he does not mind his own,
He'll find some day the bird has flown!

*Mei Sheng, 2nd cent. B.C.*

*(Soft marks: the "....' after "wife" is the 1901 Gutenberg-proofread
state; both 1898 OCR witnesses show line-end blank there. "looks down."
period present in 1898 witness 1, absent in the 1901 proofread. All
wording, the comma after "life," and "own,"/"flown!" agree across
witnesses.)*

## FINDING — the S8 chain resolved (closes the check ordered in heilmann_de_1905.md)

**The escaped-bird close is GILES'S INVENTION, born ENGLISH and born
WITH RHYME, 1898.** The chain the exhibit wanted "one step further
back" terminates here, and it does not pass through the French:

1. **French negative, total and print-verified.** Hervey 1862 does not
   contain the poem — the 97-poem body is Tang-only (in-house TOC), and
   the CXII-page introduction's five-word-metre origins discussion
   credits Qu Yuan fragments then **Li Ling and Su Wu**, no Mei Sheng,
   no gushi specimen (full 1862 text searched, chineancienne.fr .doc).
   Gautier 1867: absent (in-house TOC). Gautier 1902: absent (full TOC
   read from Gallica print scans — `toc_index/gautier_jade_1902.md`).
   The French line never carried this poem at all.
2. **Giles 1898 is Heilmann's crib, line for line, 10/10** — including
   the "Mei-Sheng" attribution (Giles follows 玉臺新詠; the 1901 History
   prints the poem as specimen (1) of Mei Shêng's five-word-metre
   priority). Grüner Rasen wächst am Ufer ← Green grows the grass upon
   the bank; … Ihr rundlicher Arm ist blendend weiß ← Her rounded arm is
   dazzling white; … Ist ihm eines Tages der Vogel entwischt! ← He'll
   find some day the bird has flown! No line of Heilmann's prose lacks a
   Giles source line; nothing in it requires any other source.
3. **The deformation anatomy, now with its motive.** In the Chinese the
   close is HER interiority (蕩子行不歸，空床難獨守). Giles (a) DELETES
   the absence line — the 蕩子 merely becomes "careless," no
   not-returning; (b) re-addresses the close as a warning about the
   HUSBAND'S interests; (c) invents the bird — and the bird arrives as
   the rhyme-fellow of "his own" (own/flown closing couplet in a poem
   rhymed aa-bb-cc-dd-ee throughout). The voice-theft is rhyme-driven.
4. **Prose→verse locates the birth exactly.** Giles's own prose
   anthology (*Gems of Chinese Literature*, 1884) does NOT contain the
   poem (negative witness, archive.org `gemsofchineselit00gilerich`):
   within Giles's corpus the poem enters only with the 1898 VERSE
   volume. The bird exists because the verse form demanded a rhyme.
5. **Then the motive is erased and the figure semanticized.** Heilmann
   1905 prose-literalizes the rhyme-tag "if he does not mind his own"
   into "wenn er nicht gut auf sein **Eigentum** achtet" — the property
   frame, implicit filler in English, becomes explicit German content.
   Bethge 1907 re-versifies Heilmann: the bird becomes the "Kleinod
   [mit] zwei Füße[n]" addressed to "Herr Gatte" — and he adds a NEW
   interiority for her ("Und trauert, daß sie nicht mehr singen darf")
   that exists in no ancestor. Deformations travel: EN verse (rhyme
   motive) → DE prose (motive erased, figure concretized) → DE verse
   (figure moralized further, ersatz voice added).

**Chain: 文選/玉臺 → Giles 1898 EN (bird born; voice stolen; rhyme
motive) → Heilmann 1905 DE prose (crib; Eigentum) → Bethge 1907 DE
verse (Kleinod; Herr Gatte) → [Mahler-era reception]. The S8 exhibit's
missing page is this file plus the French negatives.**
